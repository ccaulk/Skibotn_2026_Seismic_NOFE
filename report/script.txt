Hello everyone. Today I will present our work on applying Neural Operator Feature Embeddings, or NOFE, to seismic dimensionality reduction on the full Netherlands F3 offshore block. Our focus is on two practical questions: how well NOFE generalises as a continuous spatial operator across surveys and how latent dimension and graph construction affect geological facies separation.

Seismic surveys are huge, high-dimensional cubes. After trimming zero-padded boundary planes, the F3 volume has 601 inlines, 901 crosslines and 253 depth samples. We study two complementary tasks. First, 2D depth-log compression: mapping each 253-sample vertical trace onto a compact embedding over the 2D survey grid. Second, 3D volume reduction: compressing 3D seismic attributes across the full subsurface cube. We compare NOFE against PCA as a linear baseline and UMAP as a non-parametric manifold baseline. Our central question is whether a continuous graph-kernel operator avoids the tile-stitching seams, out-of-sample speckle and sampling-density sensitivity of standard embeddings.

Before diving into individual experiments, here is the high-level stress-test profile across six axes. On held-out trustworthiness and global geometry, NOFE-8D matches or stays close to PCA-8D, while maintaining much lower local spatial stress than both PCA and UMAP. Where NOFE pulls decisively ahead is operator generalisation. Across four independently embedded survey quadrants, NOFE keeps seam error at 0.10, whereas PCA and UMAP jump to 1.33 and 1.49. At the same time, zero-shot density transfer across 9x to 36x sampling shifts stays above 0.990 Pearson correlation.

Focusing first on our primary product, 2D depth-log compression from 253 depth samples down to 8 dimensions, the bar chart compares local spatial stress, global stress, continuity and nonlinear depth-log reconstruction. NOFE-8D achieves the lowest local stress at 0.258, compared with 0.374 for PCA and 0.791 for UMAP, as well as the lowest global stress at 0.236. It also delivers the highest MLP depth-log reconstruction R-squared at 0.339 alongside 0.953 neighbourhood continuity.

This figure gives direct visual proof of why continuous operator parameterisation matters in practice. Large seismic surveys are often processed in tiles or quadrants. Here we split F3 into four quadrants along the dashed crosshairs and embed each quadrant independently without any global alignment. PCA-8D and UMAP-8D suffer severe coordinate flips and colour discontinuities across every border, giving seam normalised RMSEs of 1.328 and 1.489. NOFE-8D stitches all four quadrants seamlessly with a mean seam error of just 0.103.

Next, we stress-test robustness to acquisition noise and irregular or variable trace spacing. On the left, as additive Gaussian noise increases from sigma 0 to 1.0, NOFE-8D keeps clean-target local stress nearly flat between 0.260 and 0.290, whereas UMAP sits near 0.79. On the right, a single NOFE-8D model trained once at stride 3x3 is evaluated zero-shot from coarse 6x6 grids, with 906 nodes and mean degree 5.6, all the way up to native 1x1 resolution with 32,436 nodes and mean degree 244.1, including a 50% random trace-dropout test. Across this entire 36x node scaling range, correlation with the native 1x1 embedding stays at or above 0.990.

A natural question is which latent dimension gives the best compression trade-off. Here we sweep d from 2 up to 32 under interleaved inline cross-validation. In panel (a), NOFE achieves the lowest local spatial stress across every dimension from 2D through 16D, reaching 0.197 at 8D and 0.136 at 16D, compared with 0.717 for UMAP. Panels (b), (c) and (f) reveal two clear Pareto elbows. At 8D, giving 31.6x compression, NOFE already achieves 88.9% 7-NN deepest-facies accuracy. Moving to 16D, or 15.8x compression, lifts depth-log reconstruction R-squared to 0.756, formation-thickness R-squared to 0.765 and 7-NN facies accuracy to 94.4%.

To see what those compressed 2D codes actually retain in physical depth, the top row shows three representative F3 traces: shallow shelf parallel beds, a dipping Chalk flank and the Zechstein salt diapir crest, shaded by ground-truth formations. At 16D, NOFE closely tracks both the major top-salt amplitude peak and the shallower unconformity transitions. In the bottom row, running completely unsupervised k-means with k equals 6 on the 16D NOFE codes recovers the salt diapir and surrounding basin geometry with 0.433 NMI, while the per-trace RMSE map highlights steep structural flanks as the hardest regions to compress.

Here we connect the 2D depth maps directly to the vertical 3D stratigraphy. Across the top row, increasing NOFE capacity from 3D at 84.3x compression to 8D and 16D progressively sharpens the Zechstein diapir and fault trends across the full 601 by 901 survey. Across the bottom row on Inline 300, notice the stark difference between a naive 3D NOFE-8D model on the centre-right and our tuned 3D NOFE-16D model on the far right: naive 2-hop averaging on oscillatory attributes creates high-frequency phase striping, whereas tuned 3D NOFE resolves coherent dipping formations.

That contrast motivated a closer look at why naive 3D NOFE blurred geological units. We traced it to three factors and introduced three targeted fixes: reducing operator depth from 2 hops to 1 hop to avoid smoothing across thin horizontal interfaces, applying vertical spatial anisotropy with pos_weights (1, 1, 2) so radius neighbourhoods follow bedding planes and replacing oscillatory wavelet phase with phase-invariant 3D texture plus normalised depth. As shown in the confusion matrices and per-class F1 bars, 3D balanced accuracy jumps from 0.368 for naive NOFE-8D to 0.536 at 16D and 0.654 at 32D, with Scruff recall quadrupling from 10% to 41% and macro-F1 reaching 0.559.

Rendering the tuned 3D NOFE-16D embeddings on an orthogonal chair-cut cube at Inline 300, Crossline 450 and Depth 160 highlights crisp dipping reflectors and the central salt dome. On the right, the class-balanced 3D PCA projection with formation centroids shows clear shallow-to-deep stratigraphic ordering while keeping held-out local spatial stress at 0.041, or 0.025 at 32D, compared with 0.427 for UMAP.

Finally, because NOFE is resolution-invariant, we can zoom into the salt diapir at full native 1x1 trace spacing—135,000 traces evaluated zero-shot without retraining. The PCA-RGB map resolves fine internal diapir lobes, and the spatial embedding gradient norm highlights salt flanks and fault boundaries with a boundary ROC-AUC of 0.667, while the per-trace KL residual flags chaotic overburden shear zones.

To summarise: first, NOFE eliminates survey stitching seams, reducing boundary error from above 1.32 down to 0.103, and transfers zero-shot across 9x to 36x density changes. Second, for 2D depth-log compression, 8D gives a strong 31.6x compact map while 16D at 15.8x is the sweet spot for waveform reconstruction and 94.4% facies accuracy. Third, for 3D facies separation, switching to 1-hop anisotropic texture graphs improves balanced accuracy by 0.286 to 0.654. As a practical trade-off, global linear probes still require sufficient latent capacity and in-domain spatial coordinates, which points toward multi-scale global anchors as natural future work.
